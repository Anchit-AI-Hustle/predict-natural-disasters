    Experimental forecast models: tested on past weather

    Our experimental models forecast extreme heat, heavy rain and wildfire weather a few days ahead. Their forecasts are on the forecasts page, marked as made in advance. Each one is recorded when it is published and graded below once its days have been measured, misses included. The models are also tested on weather forecasts that were really issued 3 and 5 days in advance; those results are in the first table.

      Full details: advance forecasts, other forecasts and missed disasters

      A forecast counts as made in advance only if we recorded it before the danger it names was known. Other forecasts are made later: about a flood that is already happening, about aftershocks after an earthquake, or as a test case written after its period began. These are real forecasts, but they do not foresee something nobody knew about. We report the two kinds separately and never add them together.

      How we label results

      For each forecast or event, we keep 4 separate labels: how severe the event was, how the forecast turned out, whether we were checking that place, and how strong the evidence is.

      Extreme eventSevere eventSignificant eventCorrectPartly correctWrong (false alarm)Missed eventMissed: not checking thereCannot be checkedNo proof it was made in time

      Precision answers this question: when we forecast an event, how often did it happen? Recall answers this one: of the significant disasters that really happened, how many had a forecast that counts, made in advance? Forecasts that are still running are left out until their period ends. A forecast based on an official warning gets no credit just because the warning already existed.
