Floods and landslides often come together

Long or very heavy rain can cause several problems at once. Rivers rise, drains overflow, hillsides become soaked and slide, and roads can close. We treat these as linked, but we check each one separately.

Where matters most

How much a hazard matters depends on where you are. A big event far away may not affect your city at all. That is why we give more weight to events near the place you choose.

How we judge forecasts

We judge a forecast only by checking it afterwards. A warning or heavy rain on its own does not count as success. The forecast is correct only if what it said, when it was locked, really happened.

How to read this

A pattern is not a prediction

Past and current patterns can tell you something about risk. They cannot tell you exactly what will happen.

Risk changes over time

New reports and official warnings can quickly change which events matter most, and where.

Hazards can come together

Floods, landslides, storms and damage to roads or power supplies can follow one another. Think about them together.
